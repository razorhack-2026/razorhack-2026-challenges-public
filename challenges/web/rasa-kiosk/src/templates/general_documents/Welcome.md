# Welcome to RASA

Welcome aboard, cadet! Here at RASA, you're part of a team. Whether you're running diagnostics on a drill platform, charting a course through the asteroid belt, or just figuring out where the coffee machine is, we're glad you're here.

This document covers the basics of your first few days. It's not long, so please read the whole thing.

## Who We Are

RASA is an interstellar mining company. We send crews and equipment to asteroids, moons, and the occasional very stubborn comet to extract the raw materials that keep civilization running: iron, nickel, platinum-group metals, water ice, and plenty more.

Our operations span:

- **Survey & Prospecting:** finding out what's worth digging up before we dig it up
- **Extraction:** the drills, the haulers, and the people who keep them running
- **Logistics:** getting ore from point A (somewhere very far away) to point B (somewhere slightly less far away)
- **Station Operations:** life support, maintenance, and making sure everyone has air to breathe
- **Systems & Security:** keeping our computers, doors, and data where they belong

## Your First Week

Every new cadet goes through the same general onboarding path:

1. **Get your badge.** Your company ID card is how you get through doors and into internal systems. Treat it like the keys to your house. See the **Workplace Safety** document for more on this. Seriously, read it.
2. **Meet your crew.** Introduce yourself to the people around you. Most problems on a mining station get solved by someone asking the person next to them.
3. **Read the documents.** Everything in this library is here for a reason. The **How To Read** guide is a good next stop.
4. **Learn the systems.** You'll be expected to connect to remote machines sooner or later. The **Connection** guide walks you through the basics.
5. **Ask questions.** Nobody expects you to know everything on day one. Or day ten.

## Our Values

**Curiosity.** Space is big and mostly unexplored. So are our systems. Poke around (responsibly).

**Teamwork.** No one mines an asteroid alone. Share what you learn.

**Safety.** A crew member who goes home in one piece is worth more than any amount of platinum.

**Learning.** This is the big one. Read the next section.

## A Note on Learning

RASA is, above all else, a place to **learn**. You were brought on not because you already know everything, but because we believe you can figure things out.

You will run into problems here that you don't immediately know how to solve. That's the point. That moment of being stuck, poking at something, trying an idea, getting it wrong, and trying again, is where the real learning happens.

We know there are tools out there that can do a lot of the thinking for you, AI assistants included. Those tools can be genuinely useful for explaining a concept, looking something up, or checking your understanding. But if you hand every problem to an AI and copy back whatever it gives you, you'll walk away with an answer and nothing else. You won't understand *why* it worked, and you won't be able to do it next time.

So please:

- **Don't feel pressured** to brute-force your way through every problem. There's no prize for finishing fastest if you learned nothing along the way.
- **Use tools to understand, not to skip.** If you look something up, make sure you can explain it afterwards.
- **It's okay to be stuck.** Take a break, talk it through with your crew, or ask a Gamemaster for a nudge.
- **Celebrate the small wins.** Figuring out one thing on your own is worth more than having ten things handed to you.

Nobody here is grading you on how many problems you solve. We care that you leave knowing more than when you arrived.
