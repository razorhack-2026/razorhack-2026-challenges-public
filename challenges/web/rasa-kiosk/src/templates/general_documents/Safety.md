# Workplace Safety

At RASA, safety is everyone's job. Space is very good at punishing small mistakes, and so are people who want into places they shouldn't be. This document covers the basics every crew member is expected to know.

## 1. Protect Your Badge

Your company ID card is the single most important piece of equipment you carry. It opens doors, logs you into systems, and tells everyone (and every machine) who you are. If someone else can copy it, they can *be* you as far as the station is concerned.

### Never photograph your badge

It's tempting to snap a picture of your shiny new ID for social media. **Don't.** A clear photo of a badge gives an outsider almost everything they need to make a convincing fake:

- **The layout and design:** colors, logos, fonts, stripe placement, and where everything sits on the card
- **Your name and role:** exactly how they're printed, so a forgery matches what a guard or scanner expects
- **Printed numbers:** many real-world access cards have their ID number printed right on them, and that number is often all you need to program a working clone

A badge doesn't have to be perfect to work. It only has to be good enough to fool whatever is checking it, whether that's a tired security guard glancing at it from across a lobby or a camera looking for the right colors and text.

### This happens in real life

- **Social media badge photos.** Security researchers and penetration testers regularly search sites like LinkedIn and Instagram for "first day!" posts showing employee badges. They use those photos to print look-alike badges that get them through front doors during authorized security tests. If they can do it, so can someone who isn't authorized.
- **Cloning proximity cards.** Many older office access cards (the common 125 kHz "prox" cards) have no encryption at all. Cheap handheld devices, and hobbyist tools like the Proxmark3, can read and copy one in seconds, sometimes just by getting close to it.
- **Long-range readers.** At the Black Hat security conference in 2013, researchers from Bishop Fox showed a modified reader, the "Tastic RFID Thief," that could quietly capture badge data from about three feet away. Walking past someone with a backpack was enough.
- **Badge-on-the-lanyard walkouts.** People wear their badges at the coffee shop, on the train, and in the grocery store. Anyone nearby can take a discreet photo, no hacking required.

### Badge rules at RASA

1. **No photos.** Not for social media, not for your family, not "just for me."
2. **Don't let outsiders see it.** If someone isn't part of RASA, they don't need a good look at your card.
3. **Take it off when you leave.** Badges stay inside work areas. Put it in a pocket or bag once you're out.
4. **Never lend it.** Not to a friend, not to a coworker who "forgot theirs." Every scan is logged under *your* name.
5. **Report lost or damaged badges immediately.** A missing badge is a security problem until it's deactivated.
6. **Be suspicious of badges that look *slightly* off.** Wrong shade of stripe, odd font, misspelled role? Say something.

## 2. Lifting Limits

Microgravity doesn't make things weightless, only *feel* weightless. Mass and momentum still apply, and an ore canister drifting at you slowly still hurts. In the gravity sections of the station:

| Situation | Limit |
|---|---|
| Solo lift | 23 kg (about 50 lb) |
| Two-person lift | 46 kg (about 100 lb) |
| Anything heavier | Use a cart, hoist, or loader drone |
| Anything labeled "LIVE ORE" | Don't. Call Extraction. |

General tips:

- Lift with your legs, not your back.
- Keep the load close to your body.
- Don't twist while carrying; turn your whole body instead.
- If you have to think about whether something is too heavy, it's too heavy.

## 3. Take Your Breaks

Breaks aren't a reward for working hard. They're part of working well. Tired people make mistakes, and on a mining station mistakes are expensive.

- **Take a short break every hour or so.** Stand up, stretch, look at something more than three feet away.
- **Eat real meals.** Snacks from the vending machine are not a food group.
- **Hydrate.** The recycled water tastes fine. Mostly.
- **Step away when you're stuck.** Some of the best solutions show up the moment you stop staring at the problem. Go for a walk, get a drink, and come back with fresh eyes.

## 4. Mental Health

Long shifts, tight quarters, and a view that's 99% black void can wear anyone down. Your mental health matters as much as your physical safety.

- Talk to someone if you're feeling overwhelmed: a crewmate, a supervisor, or a Gamemaster.
- Frustration is normal, especially when you're learning something new. It means you're pushing yourself, not that you're failing.
- It's okay to step away. The asteroid will still be there when you get back.
- Check in on your crewmates. Sometimes "How's it going?" is the most useful thing you can say all day.

## 6. Miscellaneous Advice

- **Airlocks are not doors.** They do not "just open real quick." Follow the cycle procedure every time.
- **Label your food in the shared fridge.** The unlabeled pudding *will* be eaten.
- **Don't tap on the viewport glass.** It's rated for meteor impacts, not for your nervous habits.
- **Magnetic boots off before entering the server room.** Ask IT why. Actually, don't. They'll tell you for an hour.
- **If an alarm goes off, it's not a drill until someone tells you it's a drill.**
- **Tailgating is a security violation.** Holding the door for someone without a badge is polite on Earth and a write-up up here.
- **Report anything weird.** Strange noises, strange smells, strange people asking about your badge. Better a false alarm than a real one.

Stay safe, stay curious, and keep your badge in your pocket.
